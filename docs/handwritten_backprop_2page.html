5. Mathematical & Physical Explanation of Gradient Direction

Why are all four gradients strictly negative?
The network currently predicts \(\hat{y} \approx 0.4621\), which is significantly lower than the target \(y = 2.0\). Because the error \((\hat{y} - y) = -1.5379\) is negative, increasing any of the parameters (\(w, b, v, c\)) causes \(\hat{y}\) to increase, pulling it closer to \(2.0\) and thereby decreasing the loss \(J\).
Since the mathematical gradient vector points in the direction of steepest ascent (increasing loss), a negative gradient indicates that moving in the positive parameter direction decreases loss. Taking a step in the negative gradient direction (\(-\alpha \nabla J\)) adds a positive increment to every parameter, actively driving prediction \(\hat{y}\) upward toward target \(2.0\).
